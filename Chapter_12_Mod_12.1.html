📐 02. The Core Law & Formulae

1. Rutherford Scattering Kinematics

Closest approach distance and impact parameter ($b$):

$$r_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2Ze^2}{K} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{4Ze^2}{m v^2}$$
$$b = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2 \cot(\theta/2)}{K}$$

Head-on collision: $b = 0 \implies \cot(\theta/2) = 0 \implies \theta = 180^\circ$ (Direct rebound). Large $b \implies \theta \approx 0^\circ$ (Undeviated).

2. Bohr's Postulates & Hydrogen Levels

Quantized parameters for hydrogen-like atom of atomic number $Z$:

$$L = m v_n r_n = \frac{nh}{2\pi} = n\hbar \quad (n = 1, 2, 3\dots)$$
$$r_n = \frac{n^2 h^2 \varepsilon_0}{\pi m Z e^2} = 0.529 \frac{n^2}{Z}\text{ \AA}$$
$$E_n = -\frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 h^2 n^2} = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\text{ eV}$$

Energy partition: Kinetic Energy $K = -E_n$, Potential Energy $U = 2E_n$, and Total Energy $E_n = K + U = -K$.

📝 04. Comprehensive Stepwise Derivations Blueprint
Fundamental Derivation 01

Deduction of the Distance of Closest Approach ($r_0$) in Head-On Collision

Fig 12.1A: Geiger-Marsden scattering geometry showing distance of closest approach ($r_0$), impact parameter ($b$), and scattering angle ($\theta$).

1. Consider an $\alpha$-particle of mass $m$, charge $q_1 = +2e$, initially fired from infinity with kinetic energy $K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ directly towards a target nucleus of charge $q_2 = +Ze$ in a head-on collision ($b = 0$).

2. Due to repulsive Coulomb electrostatic force, the $\alpha$-particle decelerates continuously until at a minimum distance $r_0$ (distance of closest approach), its instantaneous velocity drops to zero ($v = 0$).

3. By the Law of Conservation of Energy, the entire initial kinetic energy $K$ is transformed into electrostatic potential energy $U$ at $r_0$:

$$K = U \implies K = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r_0} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{(2e)(Ze)}{r_0}$$
$$r_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2Ze^2}{K} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{4Ze^2}{m v^2}$$

Nuclear Size Upper Bound: For a $7.7\text{ MeV}$ $\alpha$-particle fired on gold ($Z = 79$): $r_0 \approx 30\text{ fm} = 3 \times 10^{-14}\text{ m}$. Since the $\alpha$-particle turns back before touching the actual nuclear surface, the nuclear radius must be strictly less than $r_0$ ($R_{\text{nucleus}} < 10^{-14}\text{ m}$).

Foundational Derivation 02

Derivations of Bohr's Orbital Radius ($r_n$), Orbital Velocity ($v_n$), and Total Energy ($E_n$)

1. Consider an electron of mass $m$ and charge $-e$ revolving in a circular orbit of radius $r_n$ with speed $v_n$ around a nucleus of charge $+Ze$.

2. The electrostatic Coulomb force provides the necessary centripetal force:

$$\frac{m v_n^2}{r_n} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Z e^2}{r_n^2} \implies m v_n^2 r_n = \frac{Z e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \quad \text{--- [Eq. 1]}$$

3. By Bohr's Second Postulate (Angular Momentum Quantization):

$$L = m v_n r_n = \frac{nh}{2\pi} \implies v_n = \frac{nh}{2\pi m r_n} \quad \text{--- [Eq. 2]}$$

4. Substituting Eq. 2 into Eq. 1:

$$m \left( \frac{nh}{2\pi m r_n} \right)^2 r_n = \frac{Z e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \implies \frac{n^2 h^2}{4\pi^2 m r_n} = \frac{Z e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$$
$$r_n = \frac{n^2 h^2 \varepsilon_0}{\pi m Z e^2} \implies \mathbf{r_n \propto \frac{n^2}{Z}} \quad (r_1 = 0.529\text{ \AA for } H)$$

5. Substituting $r_n$ back into Eq. 2 gives Orbital Speed:

$$v_n = \frac{Z e^2}{2 \varepsilon_0 n h} \implies \mathbf{v_n \propto \frac{Z}{n}} \quad \left(v_1 = \frac{c}{137} \approx 2.18 \times 10^6\text{ m/s for } H\right)$$

6. Total Energy Derivation:

• Kinetic Energy: $K_n = \frac{1}{2}m v_n^2 = \frac{1}{8\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r_n}$.

• Potential Energy: $U_n = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r_n}$.

• Total Energy: $E_n = K_n + U_n = -\frac{1}{8\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{Ze^2}{r_n}$. Substituting $r_n$:

$$E_n = -\frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 h^2 n^2} = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\text{ eV}$$

Negative Sign Significance: Total energy is strictly negative, which proves that the electron is bound to the nucleus. An infinite energy ($E = 0$) corresponds to an unbound, completely free electron at rest ($n \to \infty$).

⚠️ 05. Red Alert Exam Trap Zone

Trap 1: Energy Inter-Relations ($K, U, E$) Signs

*Board Trap:* If total energy of an electron in an orbit is $-3.4\text{ eV}$, find its kinetic and potential energy.
• Kinetic Energy is strictly POSITIVE: $\mathbf{K = -E = +3.4\text{ eV}}$.
• Potential Energy is NEGATIVE and DOUBLE the total energy: $\mathbf{U = 2E = -6.8\text{ eV}}$.
Verify: $E = K + U = (+3.4) + (-6.8) = -3.4\text{ eV}$.

Trap 2: Classical Collapse Paradox of Rutherford Model

*Why did Rutherford's model fail?*
1. Instability Paradox: Orbiting electron has centripetal acceleration ($a = v^2/r$). According to classical Maxwellian electrodynamics, an accelerating charge radiates EM energy continuously. Its orbit should shrink into a spiral, collapsing into the nucleus within $\approx 10^{-8}\text{ s}$!
2. Continuous Spectrum Paradox: Spiral radiation should emit a continuous spectrum, contradicting the discrete line spectrum observed in experiments.

Trap 3: Orbital Frequency vs Revolution Time Scaling

Time period of orbital revolution: $$T_n = \frac{2\pi r_n}{v_n} \propto \frac{n^2}{1/n} \propto \mathbf{n^3}$$ Orbital frequency: $f_n = \frac{1}{T_n} \propto \mathbf{\frac{1}{n^3}}$. As principal quantum number $n$ increases, the time period scales with $n^3$ while velocity drops as $1/n$.

Trap 4: Applicability of Bohr's Theory

Bohr's model applies strictly to SINGLE-ELECTRON (Hydrogen-like) atoms:
• Applicable: $\text{H } (Z=1)$, $\text{He}^+ (Z=2)$, $\text{Li}^{2+} (Z=3)$, $\text{Be}^{3+} (Z=4)$.
• NOT applicable to neutral Helium ($\text{He}$), neutral Lithium, or multi-electron atoms because it completely ignores electron-electron mutual repulsion!

💡 06. Handcrafted Exemplars
Board Classic Level

Problem: A $5.0\text{ MeV}$ $\alpha$-particle is projected towards a target nucleus of Gold ($Z = 79$) in a head-on collision. Calculate the distance of closest approach. How will this distance change if the kinetic energy of the $\alpha$-particle is doubled?

Solution: Kinetic energy $K = 5.0\text{ MeV} = 5.0 \times 10^6 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J} = 8.0 \times 10^{-13}\text{ J}$, $Z = 79$.

$$r_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2Ze^2}{K} = \frac{(9 \times 10^9) \times 2 \times 79 \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19})^2}{8.0 \times 10^{-13}}$$ $$r_0 = \frac{9 \times 10^9 \times 158 \times 2.56 \times 10^{-38}}{8.0 \times 10^{-13}} = \frac{3640.32 \times 10^{-29}}{8.0 \times 10^{-13}} \approx 4.55 \times 10^{-14}\text{ m} = 45.5\text{ fm}$$

Since $r_0 \propto \frac{1}{K}$:

$$\text{If } K \text{ is doubled } (K' = 2K), \text{ the closest approach halves: } r_0' = \frac{r_0}{2} = \frac{45.5}{2} \approx 22.75\text{ fm}$$
JEE/NEET Competitive Trap

Problem: The ground state energy of a hydrogen atom is $-13.6\text{ eV}$. (a) What are the kinetic and potential energies of the electron in the second excited state? (b) If the electron jumps from the third orbit to the first orbit, calculate the frequency and wavelength of the emitted photon.

Solution:

1. "Second excited state" corresponds to principal quantum number $n = 3$ (since $n=1$ is ground state, $n=2$ is 1st excited state).

$$E_3 = -\frac{13.6}{3^2} = -\frac{13.6}{9} \approx -1.51\text{ eV}$$
$$\text{Kinetic Energy: } K = -E_3 = +1.51\text{ eV}$$ $$\text{Potential Energy: } U = 2E_3 = 2 \times (-1.51\text{ eV}) = -3.02\text{ eV}$$

2. Transition from $n = 3$ to $n = 1$ ($E_1 = -13.6\text{ eV}$):

$$\Delta E = E_3 - E_1 = -1.51 - (-13.6) = +12.09\text{ eV} = 12.09 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J} \approx 1.934 \times 10^{-18}\text{ J}$$

Frequency of emitted photon ($\nu = \Delta E/h$):

$$\nu = \frac{1.934 \times 10^{-18}\text{ J}}{6.63 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}} \approx 2.92 \times 10^{15}\text{ Hz}$$ $$\lambda = \frac{c}{\nu} = \frac{3 \times 10^8}{2.92 \times 10^{15}} \approx 1.027 \times 10^{-7}\text{ m} = 102.7\text{ nm} \quad (\text{Lyman Series, UV})$$
