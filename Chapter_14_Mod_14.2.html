⚡ 01. Complete Physical & Mathematical Explanations

A. Half-Wave Rectifier (HWR) Single Diode Design

Half-wave rectifier AC voltage ki sirf ek aadhi cycle (positive half-cycle) ko load tak pahunchata hai aur doosri aadhi cycle (negative half-cycle) ko completely block kar deta hai.

1. Positive Half-Cycle ($0 \to T/2$)

Secondary coil ka upper terminal $A$ positive $(+)$ hota hai aur lower terminal $B$ negative $(-)$ hota hai. Diode ka p-side positive se judta hai $\implies$ Diode Forward Biased hota hai (low resistance). Current load resistor $R_L$ mein top-to-bottom flow karta hai aur output voltage develop hota hai.

2. Negative Half-Cycle ($T/2 \to T$)

Polarity reverse hoti hai: terminal $A$ negative $(-)$ aur terminal $B$ positive $(+)$ ho jata hai. Diode ka p-side negative se judta hai $\implies$ Diode Reverse Biased ho jata hai (infinite resistance). Circuit mein koi current flow nahi karta ($I = 0$), aur output voltage zero rehta hai.

$$\text{Average (DC) Current: } I_{\text{dc}} = \frac{I_m}{\pi}, \qquad \text{RMS Current: } I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{I_m}{2}$$
$$\text{Maximum Efficiency: } \eta = \frac{P_{\text{dc}}}{P_{\text{ac}}} = \frac{0.406 R_L}{r_f + R_L} \approx 40.6\%, \qquad \text{Ripple Factor: } \gamma = \sqrt{\left(\frac{I_{\text{rms}}}{I_{\text{dc}}}\right)^2 - 1} \approx 1.21$$
$$\text{Output Ripple Frequency: } f_{\text{out}} = f_{\text{in}} \quad (50\text{ Hz in } \to 50\text{ Hz out}), \qquad \text{Peak Inverse Voltage (PIV)} = V_m$$

B. Full-Wave Center-Tapped Rectifier (FWR) Dual Diode + Center-Tap Transformer

Full-wave rectifier AC input ki dono cycles (positive aur negative) ko utilize karke load resistor $R_L$ mein hamesha ek hi disha mein current banaye rakhta hai.

1. Positive Half-Cycle ($0 \to T/2$)

Center-tap $T$ ($0\text{V}$) ke respect mein terminal $A$ positive $(+)$ aur terminal $B$ negative $(-)$ hota hai.
• Diode $D_1$ is Forward Biased (ON).
• Diode $D_2$ is Reverse Biased (OFF).
Current path: $A \to D_1 \to X \to R_L \to Y \to T$.

2. Negative Half-Cycle ($T/2 \to T$)

Center-tap $T$ ($0\text{V}$) ke respect mein terminal $A$ negative $(-)$ aur terminal $B$ positive $(+)$ ban jata hai.
• Diode $D_1$ is Reverse Biased (OFF).
• Diode $D_2$ is Forward Biased (ON).
Current path: $B \to D_2 \to X \to R_L \to Y \to T$.

Crucial Symmetry: Dono half-cycles mein load resistor $R_L$ ke andar current hamesha ek hi direction ($X \to Y$) mein behta hai. Isliye continuous pulsating DC output milta hai.

$$\text{Average (DC) Current: } I_{\text{dc}} = \frac{2I_m}{\pi}, \qquad \text{RMS Current: } I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{I_m}{\sqrt{2}}$$
$$\text{Maximum Efficiency: } \eta = \frac{0.812 R_L}{r_f + R_L} \approx 81.2\%, \qquad \text{Ripple Factor: } \gamma = \sqrt{\left(\frac{I_{\text{rms}}}{I_{\text{dc}}}\right)^2 - 1} \approx 0.482$$
$$\text{Output Ripple Frequency: } f_{\text{out}} = 2f_{\text{in}} \quad (50\text{ Hz in } \to 100\text{ Hz out}), \qquad \text{Peak Inverse Voltage (PIV)} = 2V_m$$

⚡ 03. Capacitor Filter Function & Smoothing

How Does a Shunt Capacitor Filter Convert Pulsating DC into Steady DC?

1. Rectifier ka direct output pure DC nahi hota; usme bohot saara AC ripple ($50\text{ Hz}$ ya $100\text{ Hz}$) maujood hota hai.

2. Capacitor ki reactance $X_C = \frac{1}{2\pi f C}$ hoti hai. DC ke liye ($f = 0$) capacitor infinite resistance offer karta hai, jabki AC ripples ke liye low impedance path offer karta hai.

3. Charging Phase: Jab pulsating DC wave $0$ se peak $V_m$ ki taraf badhti hai, diode forward biased rehta hai aur capacitor instantaneously charge hokar maximum voltage $V_m$ store kar leta hai.

4. Discharging Phase: Jab input wave peak $V_m$ se neeche girti hai, diode reverse biased hokar switch off ho jata hai. Ab load resistor $R_L$ ko current transformer nahi balki discharge hota hua capacitor supply karta hai. Agar time constant $\tau = C R_L \gg T/2$ ho, toh capacitor bohot dheere discharge hota hai. Agli cycle ka agla pulse aane se pehle voltage barely gir pata hai, jisse load par lagbhag smooth steady DC voltage milta hai!

⚠️ 05. High-Yield "Red Alert" Exam Trap Zone

Trap 1: Output Ripple Frequency for 50 Hz AC Line

*Board Question:* An input alternating current of $50\text{ Hz}$ is rectified. What is the frequency of output ripple?
• For Half-Wave Rectifier: $f_{\text{out}} = f_{\text{in}} = \mathbf{50\text{ Hz}}$. (One pulse per period $T$).
• For Full-Wave Rectifier: $f_{\text{out}} = 2f_{\text{in}} = 2 \times 50 = \mathbf{100\text{ Hz}}$. (Two pulses per period $T$).

Trap 2: Peak Inverse Voltage (PIV) Danger

*Exam Comparison:*
• Half-Wave Rectifier: $\text{PIV} = V_m$.
• Center-Tapped Full-Wave Rectifier: $\mathbf{\text{PIV} = 2V_m}$! When upper diode conducts, terminal $A$ is at $+V_m$ and lower terminal $B$ is at $-V_m$. Non-conducting diode $D_2$ faces a total reverse voltage of $(+V_m) - (-V_m) = 2V_m$. Diode must be rated for at least $2V_m$ to prevent breakdown!

Trap 3: Why Silicon & Germanium are Useless for LEDs

*Why can't Silicon ($1.1\text{ eV}$) or Germanium ($0.7\text{ eV}$) emit light?*
1. They have indirect band gaps; electron-hole recombination requires phonon momentum conservation, releasing energy as lattice vibration (heat), not light photons.
2. Their band gaps are $< 1.8\text{ eV}$; even if photons were emitted, they would fall in the invisible infrared spectrum.

Trap 4: Direction of Current in Load Resistor

Students often incorrectly assume current in $R_L$ reverses during the negative half-cycle of full-wave rectification.
In reality, diode $D_1$ guides current from $X \to Y$, and diode $D_2$ ALSO guides current from $X \to Y$. The load current is strictly unidirectional!

💡 06. Handcrafted Exemplars
Board Classic Level

Problem: A center-tapped full-wave rectifier uses two ideal diodes. The AC voltage across the entire secondary winding of the transformer is $50\text{ V (rms)}$ at $50\text{ Hz}$. If load resistance is $R_L = 500\text{ }\Omega$, determine: (i) Peak load voltage, (ii) DC output voltage, (iii) DC load current, and (iv) Output ripple frequency.

Solution: The total secondary voltage is $50\text{ V (rms)}$. In a center-tapped configuration, each diode sees only half of the secondary winding:

$$V_{\text{rms, half}} = \frac{50\text{ V}}{2} = 25\text{ V}$$

1. Peak load voltage ($V_m$):

$$V_m = \sqrt{2} \times V_{\text{rms, half}} = 1.414 \times 25\text{ V} \approx 35.35\text{ V}$$

2. DC output voltage ($V_{\text{dc}}$):

$$V_{\text{dc}} = \frac{2V_m}{\pi} = \frac{2 \times 35.35\text{ V}}{3.1416} \approx 22.51\text{ V}$$

3. DC load current ($I_{\text{dc}}$):

$$I_{\text{dc}} = \frac{V_{\text{dc}}}{R_L} = \frac{22.51\text{ V}}{500\text{ }\Omega} \approx 0.0450\text{ A} = 45.0\text{ mA}$$

4. Output ripple frequency:

$$f_{\text{out}} = 2f_{\text{in}} = 2 \times 50\text{ Hz} = 100\text{ Hz}$$
JEE/NEET Competitive Trap

Problem: An LED is constructed from a $\text{GaAs}_{1-x}\text{P}_x$ semiconductor material with a bandgap of $E_g = 1.9\text{ eV}$. (a) Calculate the wavelength of light emitted by the LED. (b) Identify the color of the emitted light. (c) What minimum forward voltage is needed to turn this LED on?

Solution: In an LED, recombination of an electron and hole releases energy equal to the bandgap: $E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \approx E_g$.

1. Wavelength of emitted light:

$$\lambda = \frac{hc}{E_g} = \frac{1240\text{ eV}\cdot\text{nm}}{1.9\text{ eV}} \approx 652.6\text{ nm} = 6526\text{ \AA}$$

2. Color identification:

$$\lambda \approx 653\text{ nm} \implies \text{Visible Red Light}$$

3. Minimum forward voltage (knee voltage):

$$V_{\text{threshold}} \approx \frac{E_g}{e} = \frac{1.9\text{ eV}}{e} = 1.9\text{ Volts}$$
