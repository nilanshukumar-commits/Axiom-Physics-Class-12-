2. Core Derivations Blueprint (Step-by-Step Board Proofs)

Blueprint 14.1: Formation of Depletion Layer, Diffusion, Drift, and Equilibrium Barrier Potential ($V_0$)

1. Due to high concentration gradients across a freshly formed p-n junction, majority holes diffuse from p to n, and majority electrons diffuse from n to p, setting up an initial Diffusion Current ($I_{\text{diff}}$).

2. As electrons diffuse into the p-side, they leave behind uncompensated, immobile positive donor ions ($N_d^+$) in the n-side. Simultaneously, diffusing holes leave behind uncompensated, immobile negative acceptor ions ($N_a^-$) in the p-side.

3. This space-charge region on either side of the junction becomes completely devoid of mobile charge carriers and is named the Depletion Layer (thickness $d \sim 10^{-6}\text{ m}$).

4. The uncompensated ions establish an internal electric field $\vec{E}_0$ directed from the n-side to the p-side. This electric field opposes further majority carrier diffusion, while sweeping thermally generated minority carriers across the junction, creating a Drift Current ($I_{\text{drift}}$) directed from n to p.

$$\text{At Thermal Equilibrium: } I_{\text{diffusion}} = I_{\text{drift}} \implies I_{\text{net}} = 0$$

5. The potential difference across the depletion layer at equilibrium is the Barrier Potential ($V_0$). Built-in electric field: $E_0 = \frac{V_0}{d} \approx \frac{0.7\text{ V}}{10^{-6}\text{ m}} = 7 \times 10^5\text{ V/m}$.

Blueprint 14.2: Full-Wave Center-Tapped Rectifier: Circuit Operation and Efficiency Formulation

1. Center-tap $T$ of the transformer secondary winding serves as the zero-volt ($0\text{V}$) reference point for load resistor $R_L$.

2. Positive Half-Cycle ($0 \le \omega t \le \pi$): Terminal $A$ is positive, and terminal $B$ is negative relative to $T$. Diode $D_1$ is forward-biased (conducts), while $D_2$ is reverse-biased (blocks). Current flows along $A \to D_1 \to R_L (\text{top to bottom}) \to T$.

3. Negative Half-Cycle ($\pi \le \omega t \le 2\pi$): Terminal $A$ is negative, and terminal $B$ is positive relative to $T$. Diode $D_1$ is reverse-biased (blocks), while $D_2$ is forward-biased (conducts). Current flows along $B \to D_2 \to R_L (\text{top to bottom}) \to T$.

4. Current flows through load $R_L$ in the identical direction during both half-cycles. Output DC current and power:

$$I_{\text{dc}} = \frac{2I_m}{\pi}, \qquad P_{\text{dc}} = I_{\text{dc}}^2 R_L = \left(\frac{2I_m}{\pi}\right)^2 R_L = \frac{4}{\pi^2} I_m^2 R_L$$ $$I_{\text{rms}} = \frac{I_m}{\sqrt{2}}, \qquad P_{\text{ac}} = I_{\text{rms}}^2 (r_f + R_L) = \frac{I_m^2}{2}(r_f + R_L)$$
$$\eta = \frac{P_{\text{dc}}}{P_{\text{ac}}} = \frac{\frac{4}{\pi^2} I_m^2 R_L}{\frac{1}{2} I_m^2 (r_f + R_L)} = \frac{8}{\pi^2}\left(\frac{R_L}{r_f + R_L}\right) \approx \frac{0.812 R_L}{r_f + R_L} \implies \eta_{\max} \approx 81.2\%$$

3. High-Yield "Red Alert" Exam Trap Matrix

Master Trap 1: Critical Conceptual Distinctions in Semiconductor Physics

Physical Scenario Common Student Misconception Correct Scientific Law Exam Consequence
Electrical Neutrality of Doped Crystal n-type is negatively charged; p-type is positively charged. Both n-type and p-type crystals are 100% NEUTRAL Donor atoms ($N_d^+$) and acceptor atoms ($N_a^-$) balance free electrons and holes exactly.
Rectifier Ripple Frequency Output ripple frequency is $50\text{ Hz}$ for all rectifiers. HWR: $f_{\text{out}} = f_{\text{in}}$; FWR: $f_{\text{out}} = 2f_{\text{in}}$ For $50\text{ Hz}$ line input: Full-Wave output ripple frequency is strictly $100\text{ Hz}$.
Photodiode Biasing Mode Operated in forward bias for maximum current. Operated strictly in REVERSE BIAS (3rd Quadrant) Fractional change in minority carriers ($\Delta p/p$) is vastly higher than majority carriers ($\Delta n/n$).
Peak Inverse Voltage (PIV) $\text{PIV} = V_m$ for all rectifier topologies. Center-tapped FWR: $\text{PIV} = 2V_m$ Non-conducting diode experiences double peak voltage: $(+V_m) - (-V_m) = 2V_m$.
Carbon vs Silicon Conductivity Carbon is a semiconductor because it is in Group 14 with Si and Ge. Diamond is an INSULATOR ($E_g \approx 5.4\text{ eV} > 3\text{ eV}$) Valence electrons in Carbon are in $n=2$ shell, bound extremely tightly to nucleus.

⚠️ Trap 2: Temperature Effect on Reverse Saturation Current

Reverse saturation current ($I_0$) is independent of applied reverse voltage, but is extremely sensitive to temperature.
• $I_0$ is generated entirely by thermal breaking of covalent bonds.
• $I_0$ doubles approximately for every $10^\circ\text{C}$ rise in temperature!

⚠️ Trap 3: Direct vs Indirect Band Gap in LEDs

Silicon ($1.1\text{ eV}$) and Germanium ($0.7\text{ eV}$) cannot be used to fabricate LEDs.
• They have indirect band gaps where recombination energy is dissipated as heat vibrations (phonons).
• Visible LEDs require direct band gap semiconductors ($\text{GaAsP}$) with $E_g \ge 1.8\text{ eV}$.

4. Handcrafted Exemplars (Analytical Numerical Solutions)

Problem 1: Doping Carrier Concentration via Mass Action Law Exemplar 01

A pure silicon crystal has intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i = 1.5 \times 10^{16}\text{ m}^{-3}$ at $300\text{ K}$. It is doped with Indium atoms at a concentration of $N_a = 4.5 \times 10^{22}\text{ m}^{-3}$. Determine: (i) the majority and minority carrier concentrations in the doped crystal, and (ii) the semiconductor type.

Solution: Indium is a trivalent dopant (acceptor impurity, $N_a$). At room temperature, all acceptor atoms are ionized:

$$\text{Majority Carrier Concentration (Holes): } n_h \approx N_a = 4.5 \times 10^{22}\text{ m}^{-3}$$

Applying the Mass Action Law ($n_e \cdot n_h = n_i^2$):

$$n_e = \frac{n_i^2}{n_h} = \frac{(1.5 \times 10^{16}\text{ m}^{-3})^2}{4.5 \times 10^{22}\text{ m}^{-3}} = \frac{2.25 \times 10^{32}}{4.5 \times 10^{22}} = \mathbf{5.0 \times 10^9\text{ m}^{-3}}$$

Because $n_h \gg n_e$ ($4.5 \times 10^{22} \gg 5.0 \times 10^9$), it is a p-type semiconductor.

Problem 2: Full-Wave Rectifier Circuit Parameters Exemplar 02

A center-tapped full-wave rectifier uses two silicon diodes with forward resistance $r_f = 15\text{ }\Omega$ each. The transformer secondary voltage between each outer terminal and center tap is $20\text{ V (rms)}$ at $50\text{ Hz}$. If load resistance is $R_L = 785\text{ }\Omega$, calculate: (i) peak load current, (ii) DC output voltage, (iii) rectification efficiency, and (iv) output ripple frequency.

Solution: $V_{\text{rms}} = 20\text{ V}$ per half winding, $r_f = 15\text{ }\Omega$, $R_L = 785\text{ }\Omega$.

$$V_m = \sqrt{2} \times V_{\text{rms}} = 1.414 \times 20\text{ V} \approx 28.28\text{ V}$$ $$\text{(i) Peak Current: } I_m = \frac{V_m}{r_f + R_L} = \frac{28.28\text{ V}}{15\text{ }\Omega + 785\text{ }\Omega} = \frac{28.28}{800} \approx 0.03535\text{ A} = \mathbf{35.35\text{ mA}}$$ $$\text{(ii) DC Voltage: } V_{\text{dc}} = \frac{2 V_m}{\pi} \left(\frac{R_L}{r_f + R_L}\right) = \frac{2 \times 28.28}{3.1416} \times \frac{785}{800} \approx 18.0 \times 0.981 = \mathbf{17.66\text{ V}}$$ $$\text{(iii) Efficiency: } \eta = \frac{0.812 \times R_L}{r_f + R_L} \times 100\% = \frac{0.812 \times 785}{800} \times 100\% \approx \mathbf{79.68\%}$$ $$\text{(iv) Ripple Frequency: } f_{\text{out}} = 2f_{\text{in}} = 2 \times 50\text{ Hz} = \mathbf{100\text{ Hz}}$$
