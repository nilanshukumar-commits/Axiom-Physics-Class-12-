Hydrogen Spectrum, Rydberg Formula & de Broglie Standing Waves

Discrete quantum transitions, Rydberg constant derivation ($R$), spectral series mapping (Lyman to Pfund), wavelength boundaries, and de Broglie's standing wave proof of quantized angular momentum ($2\pi r = n\lambda$).

⚡ 01. The Spark (Analogy)

Socho ek seedhi (ladder) ko: aap ladder ke kisi ek dande (rung) par pair rakh sakte ho, ya agle dande par ja sakte ho, lekin do dandon ke beech hawa mein pair tika kar khade nahi ho sakte!
Hydrogen atom ka electron bhi aisi hi quantum ladder par baitha hai. Jab electron kisi upar wale dande ($n_2$) se neeche wale dande ($n_1$) par chhalang lagata hai, toh dono dandon ki energy ka gap ek sharp, pure rang ke photon ($\Delta E = h\nu = hc/\lambda$) ke roop mein aakash mein chhidak deta hai. Yahi discrete lines gas ka Atomic Fingerprint (Line Emission Spectrum) banati hain! Aur Bohr ne jis angular momentum quantization ($L = n\hbar$) ko bina kisi proof ke postulate man liya tha, use 1924 mein Louis de Broglie ne ek jhatke mein prove kar diya: electron orbit asal mein ek gol bandha hua guitar string hai jisme sirf wahi orbits stable hain jahan electron ki matter wave aapas mein judkar ek perfect Standing Wave ($2\pi r = n\lambda$) bana leti hai!

📐 02. The Core Law & Spectral Formulae

1. Rydberg Formula for Spectral Lines

Wavenumber ($\bar{\nu}$) and wavelength ($\lambda$) for transition $n_2 \to n_1$ ($n_2 > n_1$):

$$\frac{1}{\lambda} = \bar{\nu} = R Z^2 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$$
$$R = \frac{m e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 c h^3} \approx 1.097 \times 10^7\text{ m}^{-1} \quad \left(\frac{1}{R} \approx 912\text{ \AA}\right)$$

For Hydrogen ($Z = 1$). Series limit (shortest wavelength $\lambda_{\min}$) occurs when electron jumps from infinity ($n_2 = \infty$). Longest wavelength ($\lambda_{\max}$) occurs when $n_2 = n_1 + 1$.

2. de Broglie Standing Wave Quantization

Circular constructive standing wave condition:

$$2\pi r_n = n \lambda \quad (n = 1, 2, 3\dots)$$
$$2\pi r_n = n \left(\frac{h}{m v_n}\right) \implies m v_n r_n = \frac{nh}{2\pi} = n\hbar$$

Orbit circumference must contain an exact integral number $n$ of de Broglie wavelengths to prevent destructive interference and energy dissipation.

📝 04. Comprehensive Stepwise Derivations Blueprint
Primary Board Derivation

Deduction of the Rydberg Formula from Bohr's Energy Level Equations

1. According to Bohr's Third Postulate, whenever an electron makes a transition from an initial higher stationary orbit of quantum number $n_2$ to a final lower orbit $n_1$ ($n_2 > n_1$), it radiates a single photon of energy:

$$\Delta E = E_{n_2} - E_{n_1} = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

2. The total quantized energy of an electron in orbit $n$ of a hydrogen-like atom of atomic number $Z$ is:

$$E_n = -\frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 h^2 n^2}$$

3. Substituting orbital energies for states $n_2$ and $n_1$:

$$\frac{hc}{\lambda} = \left( -\frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 h^2 n_2^2} \right) - \left( -\frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 h^2 n_1^2} \right) = \frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 h^2} \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$$

4. Dividing throughout by $hc$ yields the wavenumber and inverse wavelength:

$$\frac{1}{\lambda} = \bar{\nu} = \left( \frac{m e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 c h^3} \right) Z^2 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right)$$

5. Defining the fundamental constant term as the Rydberg Constant ($R$):

$$\frac{1}{\lambda} = R Z^2 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right) \quad \text{where } R = \frac{m e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 c h^3} \approx 1.097 \times 10^7\text{ m}^{-1}$$

Theoretical Crown Proof

de Broglie's Standing Wave Explanation of Bohr's Angular Momentum Quantization

Fig 12.3A: de Broglie's standing wave condition on a circular Bohr orbit ($n = 4$); standing wave fits exactly $4$ complete wavelengths into the circumference $2\pi r_4$.

1. Louis de Broglie argued that an electron revolving in a stable stationary orbit behaves like a matter wave propagating along a closed circular loop.

2. To avoid continuous destructive interference after multiple revolutions, the matter wave must form a stable circular standing wave. This requires that the circumference of the circular orbit must be an exact integral multiple of the de Broglie wavelength:

$$2\pi r_n = n \lambda \quad (n = 1, 2, 3\dots)$$

3. According to de Broglie's relation, the wavelength of an electron having momentum $p = m v_n$ is $\lambda = \frac{h}{m v_n}$. Substituting this into the standing wave condition:

$$2\pi r_n = n \left( \frac{h}{m v_n} \right)$$

4. Rearranging the terms:

$$m v_n r_n = \frac{nh}{2\pi} = n\hbar \quad (\text{Bohr's Angular Momentum Postulate Proven!})$$

Triumph of Wave Mechanics: Bohr's ad-hoc assumption ($L = n\hbar$) was revealed not to be an arbitrary rule, but the natural mathematical consequence of wave-particle duality and standing wave resonance in closed spaces!

⚠️ 05. Red Alert Exam Trap Zone

Trap 1: "Only Visible Series" in Hydrogen Spectrum

*Board Question:* Which series of hydrogen spectrum lies in the visible region?
• ONLY the Balmer Series ($n_1 = 2$) has lines in the visible spectrum ($400\text{ nm} - 700\text{ nm}$).
• Lyman ($n_1 = 1$) lies strictly in the Ultraviolet (UV) region.
• Paschen, Brackett, and Pfund all lie strictly in the Infrared (IR) region.

Trap 2: Shortest vs Longest Wavelength Formulas

*Maximum Energy = Minimum Wavelength:*
• Shortest Wavelength ($\lambda_{\min}$ / Series Limit): Largest energy transition, originating from infinity ($n_2 = \infty \implies 1/n_2^2 = 0$). $$\mathbf{\frac{1}{\lambda_{\min}} = R Z^2 \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - 0\right) = \frac{R Z^2}{n_1^2} \implies \lambda_{\min} = \frac{n_1^2}{R Z^2}}$$ • Longest Wavelength ($\lambda_{\max}$ / First Member): Smallest energy jump, originating from the immediate next rung ($n_2 = n_1 + 1$).

Trap 3: Maximum Number of Spectral Lines Emitted

If a hydrogen atom sample is excited to principal energy level $n$, how many spectral lines can be emitted in the de-excitation cascade?
Formula: $$\mathbf{N = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2} = \,^nC_2}$$ *Example:* For an electron excited to $n = 4$: $N = \frac{4 \times 3}{2} = \mathbf{6\text{ lines}}$ (3 in Lyman, 2 in Balmer, 1 in Paschen).

Trap 4: Absorption Spectrum vs Emission Spectrum

At room temperature ($300\text{ K}$), all hydrogen electrons reside exclusively in the ground state ($n = 1$).
Therefore, the room temperature Absorption Spectrum contains ONLY the Lyman Series! The Balmer series lines cannot appear in absorption at room temperature because there are no electrons in $n = 2$ to absorb light.

💡 06. Handcrafted Exemplars
Board Classic Level

Problem: Calculate the ratio of the shortest wavelength of the Lyman series to the longest wavelength of the Balmer series in the hydrogen spectrum.

Solution:

1. Shortest wavelength of Lyman series ($n_1 = 1, n_2 = \infty$):

$$\frac{1}{\lambda_{L,\min}} = R \left(\frac{1}{1^2} - \frac{1}{\infty^2}\right) = R \implies \lambda_{L,\min} = \frac{1}{R}$$

2. Longest wavelength of Balmer series ($n_1 = 2, n_2 = 3$):

$$\frac{1}{\lambda_{B,\max}} = R \left(\frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{3^2}\right) = R \left(\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{9}\right) = R \left(\frac{5}{36}\right) \implies \lambda_{B,\max} = \frac{36}{5R}$$

3. Required ratio:

$$\frac{\lambda_{L,\min}}{\lambda_{B,\max}} = \frac{1/R}{36/5R} = \frac{5}{36} \approx 0.1389$$
JEE/NEET Competitive Trap

Problem: A monochromatic radiation is absorbed by a hydrogen gas sample whose atoms are in the ground state. The excited hydrogen atoms subsequently emit radiation of six different wavelengths. (a) Find the principal quantum number $n$ of the excited state. (b) Calculate the wavelength of the absorbed photon. (c) Identify which of the emitted lines has the longest wavelength and calculate its value.

Solution:

1. Given total number of emitted lines $N = 6$:

$$N = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2} = 6 \implies n^2 - n - 12 = 0 \implies (n - 4)(n + 3) = 0 \implies \mathbf{n = 4}$$

The hydrogen atoms are excited to state $n = 4$.

2. The absorbed photon must have caused the transition from $n = 1$ to $n = 4$:

$$\frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{abs}}} = R \left( \frac{1}{1^2} - \frac{1}{4^2} \right) = R \left( 1 - \frac{1}{16} \right) = \frac{15R}{16}$$
$$\lambda_{\text{abs}} = \frac{16}{15R} = \frac{16}{15 \times (1.097 \times 10^7\text{ m}^{-1})} \approx 9.72 \times 10^{-8}\text{ m} = 97.2\text{ nm} \quad (\text{Lyman, UV})$$

3. Longest wavelength emitted corresponds to the smallest energy step among transitions down from $n=4$, which is the transition $n = 4 \to n = 3$ (Paschen first line):

$$\frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}} = R \left( \frac{1}{3^2} - \frac{1}{4^2} \right) = R \left( \frac{1}{9} - \frac{1}{16} \right) = R \left( \frac{7}{144} \right)$$
$$\lambda_{\max} = \frac{144}{7R} = \frac{144}{7 \times (1.097 \times 10^7)} \approx 1.875 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m} = 1875\text{ nm} = 1.875\text{ }\mu\text{m} \quad (\text{Infrared})$$
