Magnetic Dipole in Uniform Field & Gauss's Law

Restoring torque, oscillation SHM equation, potential energy states, and absence of isolated magnetic monopoles.

⚡ 01. The Spark (Analogy)

Socho ek simple pendulum ko: jab aap use uski mean position se thoda sa displace karke chhodte hain, toh gravity use wapas center ki taraf khinchti hai aur woh SHM karne lagta hai. Theek waise hi, jab ek compass needle ya bar magnet ko uniform magnetic field mein halka sa deflect kiya jata hai, toh magnetic field uspar ek restoring torque ($\tau = -MB\sin\theta$) lagati hai jo use wapas field ke parallel align karna chahti hai. Aur Gauss's Law for Magnetism universe ka sabse fundamental sach batata hai: agar aap kisi bhi band kamre (closed surface) mein magnet ko band kar den, toh jitni field lines andar aayengi utni hi bahar nikal jayengi, kyunki isolated North ya South pole exist hi nahi karta!

📐 02. The Core Law & Formulae

1. Torque & Potential Energy

Dipole moment $\vec{M}$ in uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$:

$$\vec{\tau} = \vec{M} \times \vec{B}, \quad U = -\vec{M}\cdot\vec{B} = -MB\cos\theta$$

Stable Equilibrium: $\theta = 0^\circ$ ($U_{\min} = -MB, \tau = 0$). Unstable Equilibrium: $\theta = 180^\circ$ ($U_{\max} = +MB, \tau = 0$).

2. Gauss's Law for Magnetism

Net magnetic flux through any closed Gaussian surface is identically zero:

$$\Phi_B = \oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = 0$$

Physical Signification: Isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist; magnetic lines are continuous closed loops.

📝 04. Stepwise Derivations Blueprint
Classic Board Derivation

Oscillation Time Period of a Magnetic Dipole in a Uniform Magnetic Field

Fig 5.2A: Magnetic needle of moment $\vec{M}$ deflected by small angle $\theta$ in uniform field $\vec{B}$, experiencing equal and opposite forces constituting a restoring couple.

1. Consider a magnetic needle of magnetic dipole moment $\vec{M}$ and moment of inertia $\mathcal{I}$ about its suspension axis, placed in a uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$.

2. When deflected through an angle $\theta$ from its equilibrium position, the deflecting couple develops an opposing restoring torque acting to decrease $\theta$:

$$\tau = -M B \sin\theta$$

3. If $\theta$ is small ($\theta \ll 1\text{ rad}$), we can approximate $\sin\theta \approx \theta$:

$$\tau \approx -M B \theta$$

4. In rotational dynamics, torque equals moment of inertia times angular acceleration ($\tau = \mathcal{I}\alpha = \mathcal{I}\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2}$):

$$\mathcal{I}\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = -M B \theta \implies \frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = -\left(\frac{M B}{\mathcal{I}}\right)\theta$$

5. Comparing with standard Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) differential equation $\frac{d^2\theta}{dt^2} = -\omega^2\theta$:

$$\omega^2 = \frac{M B}{\mathcal{I}} \implies \omega = \sqrt{\frac{M B}{\mathcal{I}}}$$

6. The time period $T$ of oscillation is given by $T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$:

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{I}}{M B}} \implies B = \frac{4\pi^2 \mathcal{I}}{M T^2}$$

Experimental Utility: This provides an exact laboratory method to calculate unknown magnetic field $B$ or magnetic dipole moment $M$ using an oscillation magnetometer.

⚠️ 05. Red Alert Exam Trap Zone

Trap 1: Stable vs Unstable Equilibrium Energy Misconception

In both positions, net torque $\tau = MB\sin\theta = 0$.
• $\theta = 0^\circ$ (Stable): $\vec{M} \parallel \vec{B} \implies U = -MB\cos 0^\circ = \mathbf{-MB}$ (Minimum energy). If deflected slightly, it oscillates back.
• $\theta = 180^\circ$ (Unstable): $\vec{M}$ antiparallel to $\vec{B} \implies U = -MB\cos 180^\circ = \mathbf{+MB}$ (Maximum energy). If disturbed, it flips over and does not return.

Trap 2: Net Force on Magnetic Dipole (Uniform vs Non-Uniform Field)

• In a Uniform Magnetic Field: Two equal and opposite forces act on the poles ($+mB$ and $-mB$). Hence $\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 0$; it experiences only a pure torque.
• In a Non-Uniform Magnetic Field: Forces at the two poles do not balance ($B_N \ne B_S$). Hence the dipole experiences BOTH a net translating force and a torque ($\vec{F}_{\text{net}} \ne 0, \vec{\tau} \ne 0$).

Trap 3: Work Done in Rotating a Dipole

External work done rotating a dipole from $\theta_1$ to $\theta_2$: $$W = U(\theta_2) - U(\theta_1) = -MB\cos\theta_2 - (-MB\cos\theta_1) = MB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)$$ • From stable to unstable equilibrium ($\theta_1 = 0^\circ \to \theta_2 = 180^\circ$): $$W = MB(1 - (-1)) = \mathbf{2MB}$$

Trap 4: Gauss's Law in Electrostatics vs Magnetism

• Electrostatics: $\oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}$ (can be non-zero because isolated positive/negative charges exist).
• Magnetism: $\oint \vec{B}\cdot d\vec{A} = \mathbf{0}$ always! If you cut a magnet into a million pieces, every single piece remains an N-S dipole pair; net magnetic pole charge inside any closed volume is strictly zero.

💡 06. Handcrafted Exemplars
Board Classic Level

Problem: A magnetic needle pivoted about its horizontal axis is free to oscillate in the magnetic meridian. It completes $30\text{ oscillations}$ in $1\text{ minute}$ at a place where the horizontal component of earth's field is $0.36\text{ G}$. What is the time period of oscillation? If the moment of inertia of the needle is $7.2 \times 10^{-6}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$, calculate its magnetic dipole moment.

Solution:

1. Frequency and Time Period:

$$T = \frac{\text{Total Time}}{\text{Number of Oscillations}} = \frac{60\text{ s}}{30} = 2.0\text{ seconds}$$

2. Given: $\mathcal{I} = 7.2 \times 10^{-6}\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$, $B = 0.36\text{ G} = 0.36 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T} = 3.6 \times 10^{-5}\text{ T}$.

3. Using period formula $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{I}}{MB}} \implies T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2\mathcal{I}}{MB}$:

$$M = \frac{4\pi^2 \mathcal{I}}{B T^2} = \frac{4 \times (9.87) \times (7.2 \times 10^{-6})}{(3.6 \times 10^{-5}) \times (2.0)^2}$$
$$M = \frac{2.842 \times 10^{-4}}{1.44 \times 10^{-4}} \approx 1.97\text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2 \quad (\text{or J/T})$$
JEE/NEET Competitive Trap

Problem: Two identical thin bar magnets, each of length $l$ and magnetic moment $M$, are joined perpendicular to each other at their ends such that unlike poles touch each other ($N_1$ touching $S_2$). The combination is suspended in a uniform magnetic field $B$. What is the net magnetic dipole moment of the combination, and what angle does the first magnet make with the field in equilibrium?

Solution:

1. Magnetic moment is a vector pointing from South to North pole.

$\vec{M}_1 = M\hat{i}$ and $\vec{M}_2 = M\hat{j}$ (at $90^\circ$ to each other).

2. Resultant magnetic dipole moment $\vec{M}_{\text{net}}$:

$$M_{\text{net}} = \sqrt{M_1^2 + M_2^2} = \sqrt{M^2 + M^2} = \sqrt{2}M$$

3. In stable equilibrium in uniform field $B$, the resultant dipole moment $\vec{M}_{\text{net}}$ aligns exactly parallel to the external field $\vec{B}$.

4. The angle between $\vec{M}_1$ and $\vec{M}_{\text{net}}$ is:

$$\tan\theta = \frac{M_2}{M_1} = \frac{M}{M} = 1 \implies \theta = 45^\circ$$

Conclusion: In equilibrium, the first magnet makes an angle of $45^\circ$ with the external field direction, and net moment is $\sqrt{2}M$.
