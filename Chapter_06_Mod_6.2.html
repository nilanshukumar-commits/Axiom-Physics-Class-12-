⚡ 01. The Spark (Analogy)

Socho ek bus mein baithe hazaron passengers (free electrons) ko: jab tak bus ruki hai, sab log aaram se baithe hain. Jaise hi bus achanak ek sharp turn leti hai (magnetic field $\vec{B}$ ke andar chalna), centrifugal force har passenger ko ek side wall ki taraf dhakel deta hai. Theek waise hi, jab ek metal rod magnetic field ke andar aage slide karti hai, toh uske andar ke free electrons par Magnetic Lorentz Force ($\vec{F}_B = -e(\vec{v}\times\vec{B})$) lagta hai, jo unhe rod ke ek end par jama kar deta hai. Isse rod ke dono ends ke beech ek potential difference ban jata hai jise Motional EMF ($\mathcal{E} = Bvl$) kehte hain! Aur jaise hi rod ek circuit complete karti hai, current flow hota hai jo rod par ek magnetic brake (retarding drag force) lagata hai—isko constant speed par chalane ke liye aapko jitna mechanical power dena padta hai, woh 100% resistor mein heat bankar nikalta hai!

📐 02. The Core Law & Formulae

1. Translating Rod Motional EMF

Straight conductor of length $l$ moving with velocity $\vec{v}$ in field $\vec{B}$:

$$\mathcal{E} = (\vec{v} \times \vec{B}) \cdot \vec{l} \implies \mathcal{E} = B v l \quad (\text{when } \vec{v} \perp \vec{B} \perp \vec{l})$$

Polarity determined by Fleming's Right-Hand Rule: Stretch Thumb ($\vec{v}$), Forefinger ($\vec{B}$), Middle finger gives positive potential end.

2. Rotating Rod & Energy Equivalence

Rod rotating with angular frequency $\omega$ about one end:

$$\mathcal{E}_{\text{rot}} = \frac{1}{2} B \omega l^2 = B f (\pi l^2) = B f A$$ $$P_{\text{mech}} = F_{\text{ext}} v = \left(\frac{B^2 l^2 v}{R}\right) v = \frac{B^2 l^2 v^2}{R} = P_{\text{Joule}}$$

Where $f = \omega / 2\pi$ is rotation frequency and $A = \pi l^2$ is the circular area swept per revolution.

📝 04. Stepwise Derivations Blueprint
Derivation A

Motional EMF across a Straight Conductor Sliding on U-Shaped Rails

Fig 6.2A: Conductor $PQ$ of length $l$ sliding rightwards with velocity $v$ in uniform inward field $\vec{B}$, enclosing area $A = lx$.

1. Consider a straight conductor $PQ$ of length $l$ free to slide smoothly on two parallel conducting rails connected through resistance $R$ in uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$ perpendicular to the rail plane.

2. Let the position of rod at time $t$ be $x$. Area enclosed by loop is $A = l x$. Instantaneous magnetic flux linked with loop:

$$\Phi_B = B A = B l x$$

3. As the conductor moves with velocity $v = \frac{dx}{dt}$, the rate of change of flux induces an EMF (by Faraday-Lenz law):

$$\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -\frac{d}{dt}(B l x) = -B l \frac{dx}{dt}$$
$$\mathcal{E} = B v l$$

4. Microscopic Origin via Lorentz Force: Each free electron inside the rod experiences downward magnetic Lorentz force $\vec{f}_m = -e(\vec{v}\times\vec{B})$. Work done in moving electron through length $l$ from $P$ to $Q$:

$$W = f_m \cdot l = (e v B) l \implies \mathcal{E} = \frac{W}{e} = B v l$$
Derivation B

Motional EMF Induced in a Conducting Rod Rotating in a Magnetic Field

1. Consider a conducting rod of length $l$ pivoted at one end $O$ and rotated with uniform angular velocity $\omega$ in a plane perpendicular to uniform magnetic field $\vec{B}$.

2. Consider an infinitesimal radial element of the rod of length $dr$ located at distance $r$ from the pivot $O$.

3. The linear velocity of this element is $v = \omega r$. Motional EMF induced across this small element $dr$ is:

$$d\mathcal{E} = B v \, dr = B (\omega r) dr = B \omega r \, dr$$

4. Total induced EMF between the center pivot $O$ and outer tip of the rod is obtained by integrating from $r = 0$ to $r = l$:

$$\mathcal{E} = \int_0^l B \omega r \, dr = B \omega \left[ \frac{r^2}{2} \right]_0^l$$
$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{1}{2} B \omega l^2 = B f (\pi l^2) = B f A$$

Geometric Meaning: $A = \pi l^2$ is the circular area swept by the rod per revolution, and $f = \omega / 2\pi$ is rotation frequency. Thus $\mathcal{E} = \frac{\Delta \Phi}{\Delta t} = \frac{B A}{T} = B A f$.

Energy Proof

Rigorous Equivalence of Mechanical Work and Electrical Dissipation

1. The induced EMF $\mathcal{E} = Bvl$ drives current $I$ through loop resistance $R$:

$$I = \frac{\mathcal{E}}{R} = \frac{B v l}{R}$$

2. Conductor carrying this current in field $\vec{B}$ experiences a retarding magnetic force opposing motion (Lenz's Law):

$$F_m = I l B = \left(\frac{B v l}{R}\right) l B = \frac{B^2 l^2 v}{R}$$

3. To pull the rod at constant velocity $v$, an external agent must exert equal and opposite forward mechanical force $F_{\text{ext}} = F_m$. Mechanical power delivered:

$$P_{\text{mech}} = F_{\text{ext}} \cdot v = \left( \frac{B^2 l^2 v}{R} \right) v = \frac{B^2 l^2 v^2}{R}$$

4. Rate of electrical Joule heat dissipated in resistor $R$:

$$P_{\text{Joule}} = I^2 R = \left( \frac{B v l}{R} \right)^2 R = \frac{B^2 v^2 l^2}{R^2} R = \frac{B^2 l^2 v^2}{R}$$
$$P_{\text{mech}} \equiv P_{\text{Joule}} = \frac{B^2 l^2 v^2}{R}$$

Thermodynamic Identity: Mechanical work performed in pulling the rod against magnetic drag is identically converted into electrical Joule heat, establishing complete conservation of energy.

⚠️ 05. Red Alert Exam Trap Zone

Trap 1: Motional EMF Across an Airplane's Wings

*Classic Question:* An airplane flies horizontally with wingspan $l$ and speed $v$. Which component of Earth's magnetic field induces an EMF across wing tips?
• The wings move horizontally: velocity vector $\vec{v}$ is horizontal.
• The horizontal component of Earth's field $B_H$ is parallel to flight $\implies \vec{v} \parallel \vec{B}_H \implies \vec{v}\times\vec{B}_H = 0$.
• Only the Vertical Component ($B_V$) is perpendicular to both wings and velocity!
$$\mathcal{E} = B_V v l = (B_E \sin\delta) v l$$

Trap 2: Metal Disc Rotating in Magnetic Field (Bicycle Wheel Spokes)

*Exam Trap:* A bicycle wheel has $N = 100$ metallic spokes of length $l$ rotating in field $B$. What is the net EMF between the axle and rim?
*Trap Answer:* $100 \times \left(\frac{1}{2}B\omega l^2\right)$.
*Correct Answer:* $\mathcal{E} = \frac{1}{2}B\omega l^2$ (Unchanged by number of spokes!) All $100$ spokes are connected in PARALLEL between the common central axle and the outer metallic rim. In parallel, equivalent voltage equals the voltage across a single spoke!

Trap 3: Train Axle Induction

When a train travels along horizontal rails, its metallic axle of length $l$ cuts the vertical component of Earth's magnetic field ($B_V$). Induced EMF is $\mathcal{E} = B_V v l$. Whether the train runs North, South, East, or West, it always cuts $B_V$.

Trap 4: Terminal Velocity of Sliding Rod under Gravity

If a rod of mass $m$ falls under gravity along vertical rails in a horizontal field $B$, upward magnetic retarding force $F_m = \frac{B^2 l^2 v}{R}$ increases as speed rises, until $F_m = mg$. The rod then reaches a steady terminal velocity: $$v_t = \frac{mg R}{B^2 l^2}$$

💡 06. Handcrafted Exemplars
Board Classic Level

Problem: A metallic rod of length $1.0\text{ m}$ is rotated with a frequency of $50\text{ rev/s}$, with one end hinged at the center and the other end at the circumference of a circular metallic ring of radius $1.0\text{ m}$, about an axis passing through the center and perpendicular to the plane of the ring. A constant uniform magnetic field of $1.0\text{ T}$ parallel to the axis is present everywhere. What is the EMF induced between the center and the metallic ring?

Solution:

1. Given: Length $l = 1.0\text{ m}$, rotation frequency $f = 50\text{ s}^{-1}$, magnetic field $B = 1.0\text{ T}$.

2. Angular velocity $\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 50 = 100\pi\text{ rad/s}$.

3. Formula for motional EMF of a rotating rod:

$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{1}{2} B \omega l^2 = B f (\pi l^2)$$
$$\mathcal{E} = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.0 \times (100\pi) \times (1.0)^2 = 50\pi\text{ Volts} \approx 157.08\text{ Volts}$$
JEE/NEET Competitive Trap

Problem: A jet plane is traveling towards west at a speed of $1800\text{ km/h}$. What is the voltage difference developed between the ends of the wing having a span of $25\text{ m}$, if the Earth’s magnetic field at the location has a magnitude of $5.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$ and the dip angle is $30^\circ$?

Solution:

1. Convert speed into SI units:

$$v = 1800 \times \frac{5}{18} = 500\text{ m/s}$$

2. Wingspan $l = 25\text{ m}$. Vertical component of Earth's magnetic field:

$$B_V = B \sin\delta = (5.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}) \times \sin 30^\circ = (5.0 \times 10^{-4}) \times 0.5 = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$$

3. Induced EMF across wingtips:

$$\mathcal{E} = B_V v l = (2.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}) \times (500\text{ m/s}) \times (25\text{ m}) = 3.125\text{ Volts}$$

Polarity Insight: By Fleming's right-hand rule (motion West, $B_V$ downwards), positive charge is pushed towards the North-facing wingtip. Thus, the North wingtip is at a higher potential.
