Semiconductors: Energy Bands & p-n Junction Diode

Energy gap classification, intrinsic vs extrinsic doping mechanisms, mass action law, space-charge depletion dynamics, barrier potential, and forward/reverse bias I-V characteristics.

⚡ 01. The Spark (Analogy)

Socho ek do-manzila imarat (two-floor building) ko: neeche ki floor (Valence Band) poori tarah bheed se bhari hai aur log move nahi kar sakte. Upar ki floor (Conduction Band) bilkul khali hai. Metals mein dono floors ke beech koi gap hi nahi hota (overlap karte hain), isliye electrons aasaani se daudte hain. Insulators mein dono floors ke beech $3\text{ to } 6\text{ metres}$ ka uncrossable gap hota hai ($E_g > 3\text{ eV}$). Lekin Semiconductor ek aisi imarat hai jahan beech ka gap sirf $1\text{ eV}$ hai (Silicon ke liye $1.1\text{ eV}$, Germanium ke liye $0.7\text{ eV}$)—room temperature par thodi si heat milte hi kuch electrons upar kood jaate hain! Jab electron upar jata hai, toh neeche ek khali jagah chhod deta hai jise hum Hole ($h^+$) kehte hain. Aur jab hum ek taraf p-type (holes ki bheed) aur doosri taraf n-type (electrons ki bheed) ko jodte hain, toh junction par ek one-way turnstile gate ban jata hai: ek disha mein current aasaani se bahega, par ulti disha mein rasta 100% block ho jayega! Yahi modern electronics ka dil—p-n Junction Diode hai!

📐 02. The Core Law & Carrier Relations

1. Semiconductor Classification & Mass Action Law

Intrinsic and Extrinsic carrier concentration equilibriums:

$$\text{Intrinsic: } n_e = n_h = n_i \quad (\text{Pure Si or Ge})$$
$$\text{Mass Action Law: } n_e \cdot n_h = n_i^2 \quad (\text{At thermal equilibrium})$$
$$\text{Total Current: } I = I_e + I_h = e A (n_e v_e + n_h v_h)$$

N-type: $n_e \approx N_d \gg n_h$ (Pentavalent dopants: As, P, Sb). P-type: $n_h \approx N_a \gg n_e$ (Trivalent dopants: In, B, Al). Despite doping, both n-type and p-type bulk crystals remain strictly electrically neutral!

2. Diode Biasing & Dynamic Resistance

Barrier reduction, threshold, and dynamic resistance:

$$\text{Forward Effective Barrier: } V_{\text{eff}} = V_0 - V$$
$$\text{Reverse Effective Barrier: } V_{\text{eff}} = V_0 + V$$
$$r_d = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta I} \quad (\text{Dynamic / AC Resistance})$$

Silicon barrier voltage $V_0 \approx 0.7\text{ V}$, Germanium $V_0 \approx 0.3\text{ V}$. Forward current in milliamperes ($\text{mA}$); reverse saturation current in microamperes ($\mu\text{A}$) due to minority carriers.

📝 04. Comprehensive Stepwise Derivations Blueprint
Physical Mechanism 01

Mechanism of Depletion Layer Formation and Equilibrium Barrier Potential ($V_0$)

Fig 14.1A: The p-n junction under open-circuit equilibrium showing space-charge depletion region and built-in electric field $E_0$.

1. Diffusion Current: Due to sharp concentration gradients, holes diffuse from p-side to n-side, and electrons diffuse from n-side to p-side across the metallurgical junction.

2. Space-Charge Layer: When an electron diffuses into the p-side, it leaves behind an uncompensated, immobile positive donor ion ($N_d^+$) in the n-region. Similarly, the diffusing hole leaves behind an immobile negative acceptor ion ($N_a^-$) in the p-region.

3. This narrow space-charge zone is devoid of all mobile charge carriers and is termed the Depletion Layer (thickness $\approx 0.1\text{ to } 1\text{ }\mu\text{m} = 10^{-6}\text{ m}$).

4. Drift Current & Dynamic Equilibrium: The immobile ions set up an internal electric field $\vec{E}_0$ directed from n-side to p-side. This field opposes further majority carrier diffusion, while accelerating thermally generated minority carriers (electrons in p-side towards n, and holes in n-side towards p), producing a Drift Current.

$$\text{At Equilibrium: } I_{\text{diffusion}} = I_{\text{drift}} \implies I_{\text{net}} = 0$$

The potential difference created across the depletion region at equilibrium is the Barrier Potential ($V_0$). Built-in electric field intensity: $E_0 = \frac{V_0}{d} \approx \frac{0.7\text{ V}}{10^{-6}\text{ m}} = \mathbf{7 \times 10^5\text{ V/m}}$ (immensely strong over atomic scales!).

⚠️ 05. Red Alert Exam Trap Zone

Trap 1: Is an n-type or p-type Semiconductor Charged?

*Classic Viva & Board Trap:* Is an n-type semiconductor negatively charged because it has excess free electrons?
*Correct Answer:* STRICTLY NO! It is 100% NEUTRAL! Every free electron donated by a neutral pentavalent phosphorus atom leaves behind a positive core nucleus ($N_d^+$). Total positive nuclear charge equals total negative electron charge. Bulk crystals of both n-type and p-type are perfectly electrically neutral.

Trap 2: Carbon vs Silicon vs Germanium Band Gaps

All elements in Group 14 have 4 valence electrons ($C, Si, Ge, Sn$). Why is Diamond an insulator while Silicon and Germanium are semiconductors?
• Carbon valence electrons reside in $n=2$ shell (close to nucleus, bound tightly): $E_g \approx \mathbf{5.4\text{ eV} > 3\text{ eV}}$ (Insulator).
• Silicon valence electrons in $n=3$ shell: $E_g \approx \mathbf{1.1\text{ eV}}$ (Semiconductor).
• Germanium in $n=4$ shell: $E_g \approx \mathbf{0.7\text{ eV}}$ (Semiconductor).

Trap 3: Reverse Saturation Current Temperature Dependence

*Board Question:* Why is reverse saturation current ($I_0$) independent of applied reverse voltage, but highly sensitive to temperature?
• Applied reverse voltage simply sweeps whatever minority carriers drift to the junction; it cannot create new ones.
• Minority carriers are generated strictly by thermal breaking of covalent bonds.
• $I_0$ doubles for approximately every $10^\circ\text{C}$ rise in temperature!

Trap 4: Static vs Dynamic Resistance

Because a p-n diode is a Non-Ohmic device, Ohm's law ($R = V/I$) does not hold as a constant:
• Static (DC) Resistance: $R_{\text{dc}} = \frac{V}{I}$ (Ratio of coordinates at an operating point).
• Dynamic (AC) Resistance: $r_{\text{ac}} = \mathbf{\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta I}}$ (Reciprocal of the slope of the forward I-V characteristic curve).

💡 06. Handcrafted Exemplars
Board Classic Level

Problem: A pure semiconductor crystal of Silicon has intrinsic carrier concentration $n_i = 1.5 \times 10^{16}\text{ m}^{-3}$ at $300\text{ K}$. It is doped with pentavalent Arsenic atoms at a density of $N_d = 5 \times 10^{22}\text{ m}^{-3}$. Calculate the equilibrium electron and hole concentrations in the doped crystal. Identify the type of semiconductor.

Solution: Since donor dopant atoms are fully ionized at room temperature:

$$n_e \approx N_d = 5.0 \times 10^{22}\text{ m}^{-3}$$

From the Mass Action Law ($n_e \cdot n_h = n_i^2$):

$$n_h = \frac{n_i^2}{n_e} = \frac{(1.5 \times 10^{16}\text{ m}^{-3})^2}{5.0 \times 10^{22}\text{ m}^{-3}} = \frac{2.25 \times 10^{32}}{5.0 \times 10^{22}}$$
$$n_h = 4.5 \times 10^9\text{ m}^{-3}$$

Because $n_e \gg n_h$ ($5 \times 10^{22} \gg 4.5 \times 10^9$), it is an n-type semiconductor.

JEE/NEET Competitive Trap

Problem: The V-I characteristic of a silicon diode is measured. In the forward bias condition, the current changes from $10\text{ mA}$ to $30\text{ mA}$ when the forward voltage changes from $0.70\text{ V}$ to $0.75\text{ V}$. In the reverse bias condition, the reverse current remains constant at $1.0\text{ }\mu\text{A}$ when the reverse voltage is varied from $2.0\text{ V}$ to $10.0\text{ V}$. Find the dynamic resistance of the diode in (a) forward bias, and (b) reverse bias.

Solution:

1. Forward Dynamic Resistance ($r_{\text{forward}} = \frac{\Delta V_f}{\Delta I_f}$):

$$\Delta V_f = 0.75\text{ V} - 0.70\text{ V} = 0.05\text{ V}$$ $$\Delta I_f = 30\text{ mA} - 10\text{ mA} = 20\text{ mA} = 20 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}$$
$$r_{\text{forward}} = \frac{0.05\text{ V}}{20 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A}} = \frac{50}{20} = \mathbf{2.5\text{ }\Omega}$$

2. Reverse Dynamic Resistance ($r_{\text{reverse}} = \frac{\Delta V_r}{\Delta I_r}$):

$$\Delta V_r = 10.0\text{ V} - 2.0\text{ V} = 8.0\text{ V}$$ $$\Delta I_r = 1.0\text{ }\mu\text{A} - 1.0\text{ }\mu\text{A} = 0\text{ A} \quad (\text{Ideal Saturation})$$
$$r_{\text{reverse}} = \frac{8.0\text{ V}}{\to 0} \to \mathbf{\infty} \quad (\text{Practically several Mega-ohms } \sim 10^7\text{ }\Omega)$$
