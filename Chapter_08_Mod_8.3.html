⚠️ 05. Red Alert Exam Trap Zone

Trap 1: The Microwave Oven Working Mechanism

*Board Question:* Why are microwaves used in microwave ovens instead of radiowaves or infrared?
• The frequency of microwaves matches the natural rotational frequency of water molecules ($\sim 2.45\text{ GHz}$).
• This produces resonant dielectric absorption, efficiently converting electromagnetic energy into thermal kinetic energy throughout the food bulk. Ordinary dry glass or ceramic containers do not absorb microwaves and stay cool.

Trap 2: X-Rays vs Gamma Rays Overlap Duality

*Exam Trap:* An X-ray and a Gamma ray have the identical wavelength $\lambda = 10^{-11}\text{ m}$. How can you distinguish between them?
• You CANNOT distinguish them by their wavelength, speed, or frequency!
• The distinction lies strictly in their ORIGIN (Production Mechanism):
— X-rays: Produced by transitions of inner-shell atomic electrons or sudden deceleration of electrons hitting a target.
— Gamma rays: Produced exclusively by nuclear de-excitation inside radioactive nuclei.

Trap 3: Greenhouse Effect & Infrared Radiation

The Earth receives short-wavelength visible and near-infrared rays from the Sun, which warm the ground. The Earth then re-emits long-wavelength infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases ($CO_2, CH_4$, water vapor) trap these longer infrared wavelengths through molecular vibrational resonance, preventing them from escaping and maintaining the Earth's average temperature.

Trap 4: Speed Invariance across the Entire Spectrum

*Board Trick Question:* Compare the speed of a $100\text{ MHz}$ radio wave with a $10^{20}\text{ Hz}$ gamma ray in vacuum.
*Correct Answer:* RATIO IS STRICTLY 1:1! All electromagnetic waves, regardless of their frequency, wavelength, intensity, or energy, travel with the exact same speed in vacuum: $$c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_0\varepsilon_0}} \approx 3.0 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$$

💡 06. Handcrafted Exemplars
Board Classic Level

Problem: Identify the electromagnetic waves whose wavelengths lie in the range (i) $10^{-10}\text{ m}$, (ii) $10^{-3}\text{ m}$, and (iii) $10^{-7}\text{ m}$. Write one source and two important uses of each.

Solution:

• (i) $\lambda = 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ ($1\text{ Å}$): X-Rays

Source: Sudden deceleration of fast electrons hitting a high atomic mass target.

Uses: (1) Medical bone fracture diagnosis, (2) Study of crystal structures (diffraction).

• (ii) $\lambda = 10^{-3}\text{ m}$ ($1\text{ mm}$): Microwaves / Infrared boundary

Source: Magnetron tube or Klystron tube oscillator.

Uses: (1) Aircraft RADAR navigation systems, (2) Microwave heating in ovens.

• (iii) $\lambda = 10^{-7}\text{ m}$ ($\approx 400 - 100\text{ nm}$): Ultraviolet Rays

Source: Very hot electric arcs or mercury vapor lamps.

Uses: (1) Water purification (bactericidal action), (2) Detection of forged signatures.

JEE/NEET Competitive Trap

Problem: A laser beam of frequency $6 \times 10^{14}\text{ Hz}$ produces an energy flux of $300\text{ W/m}^2$. What is the amplitude of the electric and magnetic fields? Find the number of photons crossing a unit area per second. (Take $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$).

Solution:

1. Intensity relation: $I = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 c E_0^2$:

$$E_0 = \sqrt{\frac{2I}{\varepsilon_0 c}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 300}{(8.854 \times 10^{-12}) \times (3 \times 10^8)}} = \sqrt{\frac{600}{2.656 \times 10^{-3}}} = \sqrt{2.259 \times 10^5} \approx 475.3\text{ V/m}$$

2. Magnetic field amplitude ($B_0$):

$$B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c} = \frac{475.3\text{ V/m}}{3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}} \approx 1.58 \times 10^{-6}\text{ T} = 1.58\text{ }\mu\text{T}$$

3. Energy of one photon: $E_{\text{photon}} = h\nu = (6.63 \times 10^{-34}) \times (6 \times 10^{14}) \approx 3.978 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}$.

Number of photons crossing unit area per second ($N$):

$$N = \frac{I}{E_{\text{photon}}} = \frac{300\text{ W/m}^2}{3.978 \times 10^{-19}\text{ J}} \approx 7.54 \times 10^{20}\text{ photons/(m}^2\cdot\text{s)}$$
