⚡ 01. The Spark (Analogy)

Imagine two magnets floating on completely frictionless ice: push them closer, and repulsion blows up exponentially, not linearly. Electrostatic force operates on a rigid, invisible line of sight connecting two point charges. Adding a third charge into the system does not alter the mutual handshake between the first two—it simply adds its own independent force vector on top.

📐 02. The Core Law & Formulae

Coulomb's Law (Point Charges)

Force between stationary charges in vacuum along their line of center:

$$\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \hat{r}_{21}$$

In medium of dielectric constant $K$: $F_{\text{med}} = \frac{F_{\text{vac}}}{K}$.

Superposition Principle

Net force is the vector sum of individual two-body pairwise forces:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \vec{F}_i = \vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_2 + \dots + \vec{F}_n$$

Scalar addition of magnitudes without directional angles leads to 100% error.

📝 04. Stepwise Derivation (Vector Symmetry)

1. Position vectors: Let point charges $q_1$ and $q_2$ be at position vectors $\vec{r}_1$ and $\vec{r}_2$. The displacement vector pointing from $q_2$ to $q_1$ is $\vec{r}_{12} = \vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2$.

2. Formulating Force: The electrostatic force exerted on $q_1$ by $q_2$ along the line of sight is:

$$\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_{12}|^2} \hat{r}_{12}$$

3. Expanding unit vector: Since $\hat{r}_{12} = \frac{\vec{r}_{12}}{|\vec{r}_{12}|} = \frac{\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|}$:

$$\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|^3} (\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2)$$

4. Action-Reaction symmetry: Inverting coordinates gives $(\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1) = -(\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2)$, proving Newton's 3rd Law:

$$\vec{F}_{21} = -\vec{F}_{12}$$
⚠️ 05. Red Alert Exam Trap Zone

Trap 1: The "Scalar Magnitude" Sum Trap

Students frequently compute forces $|F_1|$ and $|F_2|$ and add them directly ($F_1 + F_2$). Forces are vectors! Always resolve into orthogonal components ($\sum F_x \hat{i} + \sum F_y \hat{j}$) before finding the resultant.

Trap 2: Large Conducting Spheres vs Point Charges

Coulomb's Law strictly assumes point charges. For conducting spheres of finite radii, induction relocates charge centers. Like charges repel to opposite sides ($r_{\text{eff}} > r$), reducing force below Coulomb prediction.

💡 06. Handcrafted Exemplars
Foundation Level

Problem: Two fixed point charges $+4q$ and $+q$ are separated by distance $L$. At what position should a third charge $Q$ be placed so that it is in equilibrium?

Solution: Let $Q$ be at distance $x$ from $+4q$. For equilibrium:

$$\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{4q \cdot Q}{x^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q \cdot Q}{(L-x)^2} \implies \frac{2}{x} = \frac{1}{L-x}$$

$$2(L - x) = x \implies 2L = 3x \implies x = \frac{2L}{3}$$

Punchline: Distance is $2L/3$ from $+4q$. The sign and magnitude of $Q$ cancel completely, having zero effect on equilibrium position.

Advanced Symmetry Level

Problem: Five equal point charges $+q$ are fixed at 5 vertices of a regular hexagon of side $a$. Find the net force on charge $+q_0$ at the center.

Solution: By geometric inversion symmetry, diametrically opposite pairs cancel pairwise:

$$\vec{F}_1 + \vec{F}_4 = 0, \quad \vec{F}_2 + \vec{F}_5 = 0$$

The single charge across the vacant vertex remains completely unbalanced. Distance from center to vertex in a regular hexagon equals side $a$:

$$\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q \cdot q_0}{a^2} \quad \text{(directed directly toward the empty vertex)}$$
