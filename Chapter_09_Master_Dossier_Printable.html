2. Core Derivations Blueprint (Step-by-Step Board Proofs)

Blueprint 9.1: The Mirror Equation ($\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$) for a Concave Mirror

1. Consider an object $AB$ placed beyond $C$ on the principal axis of a concave mirror of small aperture, forming real inverted image $A'B'$ between $C$ and $F$.

2. From similar right-angled triangles $\triangle A'B'P$ and $\triangle ABP$: $\frac{A'B'}{AB} = \frac{B'P}{BP}$.

3. From similar triangles $\triangle A'B'F$ and $\triangle MPF$ (where $MP \perp \text{axis}$ is incident aperture height, $MP = AB$): $\frac{A'B'}{MP} = \frac{A'B'}{AB} = \frac{B'F}{FP} = \frac{B'P - FP}{FP}$.

4. Equating both expressions: $\frac{B'P}{BP} = \frac{B'P - FP}{FP}$.

5. Applying Cartesian Sign Convention: $BP = -u$, $B'P = -v$, $FP = -f$:

$$\frac{-v}{-u} = \frac{-v - (-f)}{-f} \implies \frac{v}{u} = \frac{v - f}{f} = \frac{v}{f} - 1$$

6. Dividing throughout by $v$:

$$\frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{v} \implies \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}$$

Blueprint 9.2: Refraction at a Single Spherical Curved Surface ($\frac{\mu_2}{v} - \frac{\mu_1}{u} = \frac{\mu_2 - \mu_1}{R}$)

1. Point object $O$ in medium $\mu_1$ forms image $I$ in medium $\mu_2$ across curved interface of radius $R$ and center $C$.

2. Let ray $ON$ make angle $\alpha$ with axis, refracted ray $NI$ make angle $\beta$, and normal $NC$ make angle $\gamma$.

3. Exterior angles: In $\triangle ONC$, $i = \alpha + \gamma$. In $\triangle NIC$, $\gamma = r + \beta \implies r = \gamma - \beta$.

4. By Snell's Law for paraxial rays ($\mu_1 i = \mu_2 r$): $\mu_1(\alpha + \gamma) = \mu_2(\gamma - \beta) \implies \mu_1\alpha + \mu_2\beta = (\mu_2 - \mu_1)\gamma$.

5. Substituting paraxial angles $\alpha \approx \frac{NM}{-u}$, $\beta \approx \frac{NM}{v}$, $\gamma \approx \frac{NM}{R}$:

$$\mu_1\left(\frac{NM}{-u}\right) + \mu_2\left(\frac{NM}{v}\right) = (\mu_2 - \mu_1)\left(\frac{NM}{R}\right) \implies \frac{\mu_2}{v} - \frac{\mu_1}{u} = \frac{\mu_2 - \mu_1}{R}$$

Blueprint 9.3: Deduction of the Lens Maker's Formula

1. For refraction at first surface (radius $R_1$, medium $\mu_1$ to $\mu_2$, intermediate image at $v_1$):

$$\frac{\mu_2}{v_1} - \frac{\mu_1}{u} = \frac{\mu_2 - \mu_1}{R_1} \quad \text{--- [Eq. 1]}$$

2. For refraction at second surface (radius $R_2$, medium $\mu_2$ to $\mu_1$, virtual object at $v_1$, final image at $v$):

$$\frac{\mu_1}{v} - \frac{\mu_2}{v_1} = \frac{\mu_1 - \mu_2}{R_2} = -\frac{\mu_2 - \mu_1}{R_2} \quad \text{--- [Eq. 2]}$$

3. Adding Eq. 1 and Eq. 2 ($\frac{\mu_2}{v_1}$ cancels): $\frac{\mu_1}{v} - \frac{\mu_1}{u} = (\mu_2 - \mu_1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$. Dividing by $\mu_1$:

$$\frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \left(\frac{\mu_2}{\mu_1} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right) = \frac{1}{f} \quad (\because u = \infty \implies v = f)$$

Blueprint 9.4: The Master Prism Formula ($\mu = \frac{\sin\frac{A + D_m}{2}}{\sin\frac{A}{2}}$)

1. Angle of deviation: $\delta = (i - r_1) + (e - r_2) = (i + e) - (r_1 + r_2)$.

2. In quadrilateral $AQOR$: $A + \angle QOR = 180^\circ$. In $\triangle QOR$: $r_1 + r_2 + \angle QOR = 180^\circ \implies r_1 + r_2 = A$.

3. Therefore: $\delta = i + e - A \implies i + e = A + \delta$.

4. At minimum deviation ($D_m$): $i = e$ and $r_1 = r_2 = r \implies 2r = A \implies r = A/2$. Also, $2i = A + D_m \implies i = \frac{A + D_m}{2}$.

5. Applying Snell's Law $\mu = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r}$:

$$\mu = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + D_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)}$$

3. High-Yield "Red Alert" Exam Trap Matrix

Master Trap 1: Physical Modifications across Optical Elements

Physical Action Effect on Focal Length ($f$) Effect on Image Formation Physics Rationale
Mirror immersed in water No Change ($f' = f$) Complete image, same size $f = R/2$ is pure geometry; reflection laws are medium-independent.
Glass lens immersed in water Increases 4 times ($f_w = 4 f_a$) Power drops to 25% Relative index drops: $(\mu_g/\mu_w - 1) = (9/8 - 1) = 1/8$ vs $(1.5 - 1) = 1/2$.
Glass lens in liquid ($\mu_l > \mu_g$) Sign Reverses ($f < 0$) Convex acts as Diverging $(\mu_g/\mu_l - 1)$ becomes negative; convergence/divergence inverts.
Lens cut transversely ($\perp$ axis) Doubles ($f' = 2f$) Power halves ($P' = P/2$) One curved face becomes flat ($R_2 \to \infty$); plano-convex lens forms.
Lens cut longitudinally ($\parallel$ axis) No Change ($f' = f$) Intensity drops to 50% Curvatures $R_1, R_2$ unchanged, but aperture collection area halves.
Half aperture covered black No Change Full Image Still Formed! Every uncovered point forms full image; only total brightness reduces.

⚠️ Trap 2: Microscope vs Telescope Lenses Swap

A telescope CANNOT be converted into a microscope by reversing lenses:
• Microscope requires $f_o < f_e$, both very small ($\sim \text{mm}$ to few $\text{cm}$).
• Telescope requires $f_o \gg f_e$ ($f_o \sim 100\text{ cm}$, large aperture for light gathering).

⚠️ Trap 3: Telescope Magnification vs Resolving Power

Magnification $m = f_o/f_e$ only increases angular size.
Ability to distinguish close stars is Resolving Power ($\text{RP} = \frac{D}{1.22\lambda}$), which depends strictly on objective aperture diameter $D$, not on focal lengths!

4. Handcrafted Exemplars (Analytical Numerical Solutions)

Problem 1: Lens Maker's Formula & Liquid Immersion Exemplar 01

A convex lens of glass ($\mu_g = 1.5$) has focal length $15\text{ cm}$ in air. It is immersed in a liquid of refractive index $\mu_l = 1.65$. Calculate its focal length in the liquid and state its optical nature.

Solution: In air: $\frac{1}{f_a} = (\mu_g - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right) \implies \frac{1}{15} = (1.5 - 1)\Delta R \implies \Delta R = \frac{1}{7.5}\text{ cm}^{-1}$.

In liquid ($\mu_l = 1.65$):

$$\frac{1}{f_l} = \left(\frac{\mu_g}{\mu_l} - 1\right)\Delta R = \left(\frac{1.5}{1.65} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{7.5}\right) = \left(\frac{1.5 - 1.65}{1.65}\right)\left(\frac{1}{7.5}\right) = -\frac{0.15}{1.65 \times 7.5} = -\frac{1}{82.5}$$
$$f_l = -82.5\text{ cm} \quad (\text{Behaves as a Diverging / Concave Lens})$$
Problem 2: Astronomical Telescope Parameters Exemplar 02

An astronomical telescope has an objective of focal length $100\text{ cm}$ and an eyepiece of focal length $5\text{ cm}$. Find: (i) Magnifying power in normal adjustment, (ii) Separation between lenses, and (iii) Magnifying power when the final image is formed at the near point ($D = 25\text{ cm}$).

Solution: $f_o = 100\text{ cm}, f_e = 5\text{ cm}, D = 25\text{ cm}$.

$$\text{(i) Normal Adjustment: } m = -\frac{f_o}{f_e} = -\frac{100}{5} = -20$$ $$\text{(ii) Tube Length: } L = f_o + f_e = 100 + 5 = 105\text{ cm}$$
$$\text{(iii) Near Point: } m = -\frac{f_o}{f_e}\left(1 + \frac{f_e}{D}\right) = -20\left(1 + \frac{5}{25}\right) = -20(1.2) = -24$$
